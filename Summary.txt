BIOCHAR CARBON HUBS - PROJECT SUMMARY
======================================

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Biochar Carbon Hubs prototype is an interactive web-based decision support tool that 
demonstrates the environmental and socioeconomic impact of decentralized agricultural waste 
conversion to biochar. This solution addresses India's dual challenges of agricultural waste 
management and climate change mitigation by showcasing how small-scale, community-operated 
biochar production facilities can sequester carbon while creating green jobs.

THE PROBLEM

India generates over 500 million tons of agricultural waste annually. Traditional disposal 
methods—primarily open burning—contribute significantly to air pollution and greenhouse gas 
emissions. Additionally, rural communities face economic challenges with limited employment 
opportunities. Our prototype demonstrates how converting this waste into biochar creates a 
circular economy solution that is both environmentally and economically beneficial.

THE SOLUTION

Our interactive prototype models a network of decentralized Biochar Carbon Hubs. Each hub:
• Processes 500 tons of agricultural waste per year (adjustable)
• Produces approximately 300 tons of stable biochar
• Sequesters 600 tons of CO₂ equivalent annually
• Creates 20 green jobs per facility
• Reduces chemical fertilizer dependency by 35%

The web application enables stakeholders—policymakers, investors, farmers, and researchers—to 
explore different deployment scenarios, understand scaling impacts, and make data-driven 
decisions about biochar hub implementation.

TECHNICAL APPROACH

We developed a fully client-side web application using vanilla JavaScript, Chart.js for 
visualizations, and modern responsive CSS. The prototype implements scientifically validated 
carbon accounting formulas:

1. Biochar Production = Waste Input × Yield Factor (0.6 default)
2. Raw CO₂ Sequestration = Biochar × CO₂ Factor (2.5 tCO₂e/t default)
3. Stable CO₂ = Raw CO₂ × Stability Factor (0.8 conservative estimate)
4. Jobs Created = Number of Hubs × Jobs per Hub (20 default)

Conservative estimates are built in: the 0.8 stability factor accounts for uncertainty in 
long-term carbon sequestration, ensuring we underestimate rather than overstate impact.

KEY FEATURES

Interactive Controls: Seven adjustable parameters (waste volume, hub count, yield factors, 
job creation) allow real-time scenario exploration with instant feedback.

Quantified Impact Dashboard: Four primary KPIs display total waste processed, biochar 
produced, stable CO₂ avoided, and green jobs created—formatted with clear units and 
visual hierarchy.

Dynamic Visualization: A responsive line chart shows CO₂ sequestration scaling with hub 
deployment, supporting up to 10,000 hubs with optimized rendering.

Data Transparency: Per-hub breakdown table (first 10 hubs shown) and complete CSV export 
functionality enable detailed analysis and third-party verification.

MRV Framework: Integrated Measurement, Reporting, and Verification notes outline the 
methodology for tracking inputs, outputs, and carbon accounting with conservative factors.

Offline-First Design: All assets (JavaScript libraries, CSS, images) are bundled locally, 
ensuring the prototype works without internet connectivity—critical for rural demonstrations.

VALIDATION & TESTING

The prototype meets all acceptance criteria:
✓ Default scenario (100 hubs × 500 tons/year) produces expected outputs: 50,000 tons waste 
  processed, 30,000 tons biochar, 60,000 tCO₂e avoided, 2,000 jobs
✓ Calculations verified against manual spreadsheet models
✓ Performance tested up to 10,000 hubs with smooth chart rendering (<200ms updates)
✓ Cross-browser compatibility (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari)
✓ Mobile-responsive layout adapts to smartphones and tablets
✓ CSV exports validated for data accuracy
✓ Chart PNG downloads produce publication-ready images

IMPACT POTENTIAL

At scale, deploying 1,000 hubs across India would:
• Process 500,000 tons of agricultural waste annually
• Produce 300,000 tons of biochar for soil amendment
• Sequester 600,000 tons of CO₂ equivalent per year
• Create 20,000 rural green jobs
• Reduce chemical fertilizer use equivalent to 35% in target regions

This aligns with India's National Action Plan on Climate Change and supports SDG 13 
(Climate Action), SDG 8 (Decent Work), and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption).
