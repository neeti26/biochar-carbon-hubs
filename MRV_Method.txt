MEASUREMENT, REPORTING & VERIFICATION (MRV) METHOD
==================================================
Biochar Carbon Hubs - Carbon Accounting Framework

VERSION: 1.0
DATE: November 2025
SCOPE: Decentralized biochar production from agricultural waste


1. INTRODUCTION & OBJECTIVES
-----------------------------

This MRV framework establishes standardized procedures for measuring, reporting, and 
verifying carbon sequestration claims from Biochar Carbon Hub operations. The methodology 
prioritizes conservative estimates, transparency, and third-party auditability to ensure 
credible carbon accounting aligned with international standards (ISO 14064, Verra VCS).

Primary Objectives:
• Quantify net CO₂ sequestration with ≥80% confidence
• Track operational inputs/outputs for continuous improvement
• Enable third-party verification for carbon credit certification
• Maintain transparent, auditable records


2. MEASUREMENT PROTOCOLS
------------------------

2.1 INPUT MEASUREMENT (Agricultural Waste)

Parameter: Total waste mass (kg or tons)
Method: Calibrated platform scales (±1% accuracy)
Frequency: Every batch intake
Recording: Date, source farm/cooperative, waste type, moisture content

Moisture Content Determination:
• Sample collection: Representative 1-2 kg sample per batch
• Equipment: Moisture analyzer (gravimetric or infrared)
• Correction: Calculate dry matter weight = wet weight × (1 - moisture %)
• Rationale: Carbon content calculations require dry mass basis

2.2 OUTPUT MEASUREMENT (Biochar Production)

Parameter: Biochar mass (kg or tons)
Method: Calibrated platform scales (±1% accuracy)
Frequency: Post-production, after cooling to ambient temperature
Recording: Date, batch ID, production temperature, residence time

Quality Checks:
• Visual inspection for consistent charring (no visible organic matter)
• Random sampling for lab analysis (10% of batches minimum)

2.3 CARBON CONTENT ANALYSIS

Laboratory Testing (Third-Party Certified Lab):
• Proximate analysis: Fixed carbon, volatile matter, ash content
• Ultimate analysis: Total carbon (C), hydrogen (H), oxygen (O), nitrogen (N)
• Frequency: Quarterly or every 50 batches (whichever is more frequent)
• Standard: ASTM D1762 or equivalent

Conservative Carbon Factor:
• Use lower 95% confidence interval from lab results
• Default assumption: 2.5 tCO₂e per ton biochar (pending local calibration)
• Adjust based on feedstock-specific testing


3. REPORTING PROCEDURES
-----------------------

3.1 BATCH-LEVEL REPORTING (Weekly)

Required Data Points:
1. Batch ID and date
2. Waste input (dry tons)
3. Waste source and type (rice husk, wheat straw, corn cobs, etc.)
4. Biochar output (tons)
5. Yield factor (biochar/waste ratio)
6. Production parameters (temperature, time)
7. Equipment operation hours
8. Operator initials

Format: Digital spreadsheet or mobile app entry
Storage: Cloud backup with local redundancy

3.2 MONTHLY SUMMARY REPORTS

Aggregated Metrics:
• Total waste processed (tons)
• Total biochar produced (tons)
• Average yield factor
• Cumulative CO₂ sequestered (conservative estimate)
• Equipment downtime and maintenance hours
• Safety incidents (if any)

Distribution: Hub manager, regional coordinator, verification body

3.3 ANNUAL COMPREHENSIVE REPORT

Includes:
• Full batch logs (CSV export)
• Lab analysis certificates (PDFs)
• Quality control deviations and corrective actions
• Carbon accounting methodology statement
• Third-party verification statement (if completed)
• Photographic evidence of operations


4. VERIFICATION FRAMEWORK
-------------------------

4.1 INTERNAL VERIFICATION (Continuous)

Responsibilities: Hub manager and regional supervisor
Activities:
• Daily visual inspection of scales and equipment calibration
• Monthly cross-check of random batch records against physical logs
• Quarterly review of calculation formulas and data entry accuracy

4.2 EXTERNAL VERIFICATION (Annual)

Verifier Requirements:
• Independent third-party certification body (ISO 14065 accredited preferred)
• Experience in carbon offset verification

Verification Scope:
• On-site audit: Random sampling of 20% of batches
• Document review: Complete batch logs, lab certificates, calibration records
• Interviews: Operators, managers, farmers
• Physical inspection: Scales, storage facilities, feedstock/biochar stockpiles
• Recalculation: Verify carbon accounting formulas and stability factors

Verification Opinion: Reasonable assurance statement (±5% materiality threshold)

4.3 TECHNOLOGY-ENABLED VERIFICATION

Future Enhancements:
• IoT sensors for automated weight logging (eliminating manual entry errors)
• Blockchain timestamping of batch records (ensuring data immutability)
• Remote camera monitoring for visual verification
• QR code tracking from feedstock to biochar application


5. CARBON ACCOUNTING FORMULAS
------------------------------

5.1 CORE CALCULATIONS

Step 1: Dry Waste Input
  DryWaste = WetWaste × (1 - MoistureContent)

Step 2: Biochar Production
  Biochar = DryWaste × YieldFactor
  (YieldFactor default: 0.6; range: 0.5-0.7 depending on feedstock and pyrolysis conditions)

Step 3: Raw CO₂ Sequestration
  CO₂_raw = Biochar × CarbonFactor
  (CarbonFactor default: 2.5 tCO₂e/t biochar; adjust based on lab results)

Step 4: Stable CO₂ Sequestration (Conservative)
  CO₂_stable = CO₂_raw × StabilityFactor
  (StabilityFactor default: 0.8; accounts for potential mineralization over 100-year period)

Step 5: Net CO₂ (Accounting for Emissions)
  CO₂_net = CO₂_stable - Emissions_transport - Emissions_production
  (Production emissions estimated at 5-10% of gross sequestration for biomass pyrolysis)

5.2 CONSERVATIVE ADJUSTMENTS

Uncertainty Discount:
• Apply 20% reduction via stability factor (0.8) until >5 years field monitoring confirms 
  biochar persistence in soil
• Buffer pool contribution: 10% of credits held in reserve for potential reversals 
  (aligned with Verra VCS Buffer Pool mechanism)

Baseline Scenario:
• Counterfactual: Assume waste would decompose aerobically (not burned), avoiding 
  methane emissions credit (conservative choice)


6. DATA MANAGEMENT & TRANSPARENCY
----------------------------------

6.1 DATA COLLECTION TOOLS

• Mobile app (Android/iOS): Field data entry with offline sync
• Cloud database: Secure storage with role-based access control
• CSV exports: Monthly automated generation for auditors
• API integration: Future connection to carbon registries

6.2 PUBLIC TRANSPARENCY

• Annual public disclosure of aggregated metrics (not farm-specific data)
• Open-source carbon accounting tool (this prototype)
• Community presentations to farmer cooperatives and local government

6.3 DATA RETENTION

• Batch logs: 10 years minimum
• Lab certificates: Permanent
• Verification reports: Permanent
• Calibration records: 5 years


7. QUALITY ASSURANCE / QUALITY CONTROL (QA/QC)
-----------------------------------------------

QA Measures:
• Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for all measurement activities
• Operator training and certification (annual refresher)
• Equipment calibration by certified technician (annual, with quarterly checks)

QC Checks:
• Duplicate measurements: 10% of batches weighed twice by different operators
• Lab inter-comparison: Split samples sent to two labs annually
• Data validation: Automated range checks (e.g., yield factor must be 0.3-0.9)


8. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
-------------------------

• Annual methodology review based on latest IPCC guidelines and peer-reviewed literature
• Incorporation of field monitoring data (soil carbon tests) to refine stability factor
• Stakeholder feedback integration (farmers, verifiers, buyers)


9. LIMITATIONS & DISCLAIMERS
----------------------------

Current Limitations:
• Stability factor (0.8) is a conservative assumption; actual biochar stability may be 
  higher (>95% over centuries) but requires long-term field validation
• CO₂ factor (2.5 tCO₂e/t) is based on literature averages; feedstock-specific values 
  may vary by ±20%
• This framework does not yet account for co-benefits (soil health, water retention, 
  reduced fertilizer emissions) to maintain focus on direct carbon sequestration

Future Enhancements:
• Soil carbon monitoring at application sites (multi-year field trials)
• Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) to quantify full carbon footprint
• Integration with remote sensing for land use verification
